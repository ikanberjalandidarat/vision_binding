Four stages, with evidence gates

No manual annotations are required. Simulator metadata supplies task construction, rewards and evaluation, never policy inputs. Execution completion is separate from passing a learning gate. Stage 1 is implemented below; stages 2–4 are the agreed experimental design, not yet an automatically runnable end-to-end pipeline.

Run stage 1

Each arm trains for at most 128 episodes on a fixed training family with 1.5-block starts. It evaluates initialization and checkpoints 32, 64, 96 and 128 on the same 16 near-start development tasks drawn across held-out families. The panel includes 12 present and 4 absent tasks when available. Both sampled and greedy evaluations run; sampling uses a separate generator reset per task to keep comparisons reproducible without perturbing training RNGs.

Final validation returns to the original starting position. Near-start probes have an explicit separate reset argument and episode prefix, and are stored in probes.json. They cannot silently become original-start results. Goal-relative near starts are privileged and simplify target selection; passing this experiment does not demonstrate binding.

The two movement-duration arms repeat actions for 2 or 4 simulator ticks. Both use the same 96-decision limit; the longer-duration arm consequently has a larger maximum simulation-time budget. Compare episode ticks as well as success. Stop remains immediate. No policy or vision architecture is changed.

Commit and push the local changes yourself first. The submitter creates a fresh suite, runs a two-training-episode simulator smoke, then submits four arms with at most two concurrent. A smoke execution pass releases the pilot; it is not a learning pass. Use --dry-run to inspect submission commands. No later scientific stages are automatically submitted.

What constitutes progress?

learning-gate.json requires the final two checkpoints to reach at least 80% success separately for present and absent targets in both action-selection modes, with at least a 20-percentage-point present-target improvement over initialization in each mode. These are predeclared development thresholds, not statistical guarantees. Require replication across seeds before progressing. A high-performing untrained baseline calls for a more discriminating task, not a learning claim.

Outputs include checkpoint-00000.pt and later checkpoints, training.json, probes.json, final original-start validation.json, per-episode raw frames, movement GIFs, trajectories and evaluator telemetry. Failure leaves difficulty fixed and stops at the budget cap. No automatic distance advancement occurs.

Connecting Q/K/V to the learned policy

The policy consumes frozen visual features, not generated language answers. Later intervention experiments must insert hooks into that exact encoder forward, then feed the recomputed features through the same recurrent policy. For immediate action comparisons, hold the incoming recurrent state and previous action fixed. For rollout comparisons, reset the simulator to matched starts and allow states to evolve naturally; never reuse stale masks after camera motion.

Geometry-derived regions are privileged diagnostic patch locations, not model detections. A donor color swap tests causal influence but may deliberately make behavior incorrect. A same-scene clean donor restoring corrupted information tests rescue. Rescue-training pairs must remain in the training split; development data select settings, and untouched test scenes measure donor-free performance. Under full occlusion, use temporal memory rather than assume a single frame contains the hidden information.

Grad-CAM and attention can illustrate these experiments, but neither substitutes for controlled interventions or actual correct arrival. Local synthetic tests verify orchestration only; the simulator smoke and subsequent Oscar results remain required.
