Temporal dependence audit of the released predictive coder.
(a) A frozen checkpoint was evaluated on a ten-frame original Minecraft validation clip. In two separate forward passes, only input frame 5 or frame 9 was replaced with the corresponding frame from one donor episode; frames 0-4 and all other frames were unchanged. The output at index 4 (evaluated as a prediction of frame 5) and the latent at index 4 were compared with the original run. An identical-input repeat served as a numerical control. (b) The released implementation applies F.layer_norm(f, f.shape[1:]) to a tensor with dimensions [B,T,C,H,W], pooling normalization statistics across time. This provides a source-supported route for future information to affect earlier states despite causal attention; the diagnostic did not isolate the contribution of normalization through ablation. (c) Both substitutions changed the earlier output and latent, whereas the identical repeat changed neither. Bars show mean absolute changes, not prediction errors. The output and latent use different units. Results describe one clip and one donor episode; no confidence intervals or population significance are claimed. (d) The prepared spatial protocol keeps only the final latent of a window ending at the evaluated position. No frames after that endpoint enter the model. This avoids future input at the readout endpoint, but does not establish that the original training was causal or reproduce the full published protocol.

Consequences: the earlier RGB MSE of 0.01293 versus a copy-previous baseline of 0.02271 is a full-clip output comparison with target access, not validated causal forecasting performance. This audit does not quantify how much target access contributed to that MSE, determine whether cognitive maps exist, or invalidate every analysis in the paper.
